Built for one truck in particular

Generic vehicle apps don't know your truck has six switches at specific amperages, or that airing down matters. These are the parts that only make sense for a Raptor.

AUX capacity planning

Factory-accurate switch layouts per generation. Assign mods to switches, record amp draw, and see current load, planned load from your wishlist, and remaining headroom against the fuse rating.

Flagged near limit past 80% of the fuse and over above it, while it's still on the wishlist.

Forum-ready build sheets

Turn your installed mods into a list you can paste straight into a forum post or signature: BBCode, Markdown, or plain text, grouped by category.

Your VIN, purchase price, and insurance details are never included, and prices are off by default.

Unknown is reported, not assumed

A switch with an unrecorded amp draw reads unknown, not fine. Trip totals say how many outings lack odometer readings instead of counting those miles as zero.

A forecast with too little mileage history is withheld rather than guessed.

Your build, ready to post

The list you already maintain by hand in a forum signature, generated from records you were keeping anyway, with each mod's AUX switch alongside it.

What it tracks

Capture what happens to the truck, then get answers back out of it.

  • ModificationsPart, brand, vendor, cost, install date and mileage, photos, receipts, and wiring notes.
  • MaintenanceService records with receipts, who did the work, and intervals from Ford's factory schedule or your own.
  • ForecastingProjects each interval against how much you actually drive, so "due in 3,400 mi" becomes a date.
  • Fuel economyMeasured full tank to full tank, with partial and missed fill-ups handled, against the EPA rating.
  • Trail log & air-down cardOutings with terrain, tire set, and pressures. Each tire set gets a printable card of what you ran.
  • Cost of ownershipPurchase, loan or lease, mods, maintenance, and fuel, down to cost per mile.
  • Wishlist & budgetPlanned purchases against a monthly budget, with the electrical load checked before you buy.
  • RecallsNHTSA campaigns for your model year. You mark which affect your truck and which are repaired.
  • Warranty & paperworkWarranties, registration, inspection, insurance, and the window sticker, with expiry reminders.
  • Specs from the manualFluid capacities, part numbers, and wheel torque for your generation, with Ford's owner's manual cited.
  • ReportsA build sheet PDF, and a vehicle history for selling the truck with every service and odometer reading.
  • Your unitsMiles or kilometers, gallons or liters, mpg or L/100 km, psi, kPa, or bar, and your currency.
  • Import from other appsCSV exports from Fuelly, Drivvo, and Simply Auto are recognized, previewed, and deduplicated.
  • Backups off the boxNightly backups, copied to a NAS folder, a WebDAV server, or S3-compatible storage.
  • UndoDeletes go to a trash for 30 days, photos and all, with an Undo button right after.
  • On your phoneInstalls to the home screen, with a Quick Add screen for logging a fill-up at the pump.

Run it yourself

Docker is the quickest way. The image is built for amd64 and arm64, so the same steps work on a server, a VM, or a Raspberry Pi.

  1. A 64-bit machine: a Linux server or VM, a spare mini PC, or a Raspberry Pi 3, 4, 5, or Zero 2 W on 64-bit Raspberry Pi OS.
  2. Set two secrets in .env: a session secret and the password for your first sign-in.
  3. Set a real password under Settings → Account once you're in.
mkdir raptortracker && cd raptortracker
curl -fsSLO https://raw.githubusercontent.com/breed007/RaptorTracker/main/docker-compose.yml
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/breed007/RaptorTracker/main/.env.example -o .env

# Edit .env: set SESSION_SECRET and ADMIN_PASSWORD
docker compose up -d

Without Docker, sudo bash install.sh from a clone sets up Node, a service, and nginx or Apache on Ubuntu, Debian, Raspberry Pi OS, or the RHEL family. See the Docker and Linux guides, and Running on a Raspberry Pi.

What this is, plainly

  • Self-hosted and single-owner. One password, your server, your database file. There is no cloud service and no account to create. Your records stay on your server. On its own it calls out only for NHTSA lookups and a daily check for new releases, which you can turn off; email, webhooks, and off-box backups go where you point them. The README lists every request.
  • Your data is yours. Everything lives in one SQLite file plus an uploads folder. The app backs both up as a ZIP every night, and any record type exports to CSV.
  • It expects you to run a server. If you've never used SSH, this will be a real afternoon. Docker or the installer does the heavy lifting, but it's still a machine you have to maintain.
  • Upgrades keep your data. Any earlier release's database, 0.x included, migrates forward on first start, and within 1.x nothing you rely on breaks.
  • Not affiliated with Ford. Reference specs come from public sources and Ford's free owner's manuals, with the manual named. Workshop-manual torque values and wiring diagrams are not included.
  • MIT licensed. Fork it, change it, run it however you want.
